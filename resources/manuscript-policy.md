# Manuscript editorial policy

Apply this policy whenever drafting, revising, outlining or reviewing a manuscript, including methods, captions, tables and references. Apply it implicitly: deliver the requested work without announcing the policy, quoting these instructions or adding a compliance preamble. Ordinary greetings and unrelated research questions do not need a manuscript checklist. Keep generated text in English.

## Writing and narrative

1. Establish the research difficulty with a concise, specific opening. Explain the gap, the proposed contribution, how it works and why the design choices were made, then the evidence, conclusions and future work. Make the narrative understandable to a reader unfamiliar with the specialized method, without condescension or exaggerated claims.
2. State the same precise, evidence-supported novelty in three places: the abstract, the end of the introduction and the conclusion. Say what is proposed, how it differs from relevant prior work and what the evidence supports. Consistency matters more than identical wording. When novelty has not been established, leave a specific drafting placeholder or ask for the missing contribution; never invent one.
3. Immediately after the contribution statement at the end of the introduction, add a short paragraph explaining the article's structure. Keep that roadmap consistent with the actual sections.
4. Use impersonal scientific prose by default, with clear subjects and verbs. Avoid first-person statements in manuscript prose. Describe executed methods in the appropriate past tense and proposed methods as planned.
5. Briefly explain a technique or specialized concept before naming or using it. Expand an acronym at first use in the abstract and again in the main text, which must stand independently. Define unfamiliar terms before using them in tables or figures. Replace forward-dependent concepts with a short, high-level explanation until their formal introduction.
6. For an empirical article, start from Abstract, Introduction, Related Work, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion and Conclusion. Adapt section names, order, separate limitations, ethics and appendices to the contribution type and the current official venue instructions. Theory, dataset, systems and survey articles retain the structure appropriate to their evidence.
7. State material limitations concisely and constructively: identify the scope, explain its effect on interpretation and give a realistic mitigation or future direction when supported. Preserve relevant negative results, uncertainty and threats to validity. Do not disguise weaknesses as strengths or omit limitations that could change a reader's conclusion.
8. Use commas, colons, semicolons or parentheses for sentence breaks instead of em dashes or en dashes. Preserve necessary hyphens in identifiers, scientific notation, quoted evidence and code.

## Figures

Introduce and explain each figure in the text before its first appearance. Place it immediately below the first mention in the editable manuscript where possible; a venue template or typesetting engine may control final float placement. Explain what the figure shows and the interpretation supported by it. Keep captions short and self-contained, clarifying symbols or elements not explained in the surrounding text.

Design plots at their final printed size with legible axes, units, legends and labels. Match plot text approximately to the article's body text. Use a pleasant, distinct complementary palette and check a grayscale rendering. Distinguish series with luminance, line styles or markers as needed; use textures only when simpler encodings are insufficient. Verify print and grayscale readability from a rendered artifact when rendering tools are available; otherwise describe that check as pending.

## Tables

Introduce the table in the text and explain its purpose, terms, units and interpretation. Use only names already defined in the text. Keep captions short, clarifying elements not explained nearby. Prefer a simple grid without merged cells. Use bold sparingly for relevant values or categories and state what the emphasis means; an observed best mean does not establish statistical significance. Explain uncertainty and sample size where applicable.

## References

Verify every reference before presenting it as verified. For a DOI reference, confirm that the DOI resolves and retrieve publisher or registration-agency metadata, such as Crossref or DataCite. Match the article title, authors, journal, volume and page range or article number against that record. Compare titles after normalizing formatting, whitespace, Unicode and markup only; a different title is a mismatch. Allow initials and full-name variants for authors when the remaining identity agrees; ambiguous surnames or incompatible authors require review. A resolving DOI alone does not verify a bibliography. Distinguish an absent field in the record from a match, and handle online-first articles without inventing volume or pages.

For a work without a DOI, search the exact title and author, using Google when available, and confirm a credible publisher, institutional repository or library record. A search result alone does not establish identity. Record the source and verification date for checks performed. If external lookup is unavailable or inconclusive, mark the reference or fields as verification pending rather than claiming a search or successful match. Curated-note identity checks are not full bibliographic verification.

Cite the original work for an attributed method, finding or dataset. Include recent relevant work near the manuscript's working year alongside foundational sources; relevance and evidence determine inclusion. If the target journal is known, search for two or three recent related articles from that journal and cite those that substantively support the manuscript. Do not add irrelevant journal citations to meet a quota, fabricate candidate references or replace original sources with recent secondary accounts.

## Final editorial pass

Before returning manuscript text, check the three novelty statements, introduction roadmap, definition order, acronym expansions, section fit, figure and table introductions/captions, print-readability evidence, citation identity and honest limitations. Revise defects that can be resolved from available evidence. Report only unresolved issues that affect the requested deliverable; keep the policy itself implicit.
